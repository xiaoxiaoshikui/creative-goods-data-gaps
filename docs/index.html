Who is missing from creative economy statistics?

Least developed countries have no published creative-goods export value for about a third of the years since 2002, and some are not listed at all. This page shows which economies are missing, how the definition of creative goods shifts with each revision of the Harmonized System, and what this means for creative economy indicators.

04The definition itself moves with each HS revision

UNCTAD defines creative goods as a list of 6-digit Harmonized System (HS) codes, and the HS is revised about every five years. Using the UN Statistics Division correlation tables, each creative code is traced to the next edition. A code that is split, merged or re-linked is a point where a time series can break.

06What this means for creative economy measurement

  1. Read totals for small and least developed economies with care. Group totals in UNCTADstat are estimates, and the LDC total is not published for 2002, 2003 or 2024. A change over time can reflect which economies reported rather than what was traded.
  2. Partner data could fill part of the gap. When an economy publishes no value, its partners' imports from it are usually still recorded. A partner-reported series, clearly flagged as such, could put some of the missing economies back on the map. Its accuracy for small exporters would need to be tested first.
  3. Publish the concordance with the indicator. In HS 2022, every code for video games, recorded media and toys kept its number but gained a partial link to new headings for e-waste (85.49) or display modules (85.24), and UNCTAD moved works of art over 100 years old from visual arts to cultural heritage. A code-level concordance released with each update would show which breaks in a series come from the classification rather than from trade.
  4. Goods are only part of the creative economy. Music, audiovisual and publishing increasingly move as services and digital flows. Indicators of the effects of AI and digital platforms will need other sources, such as platform and marketplace data, held to the same standard of coverage. The companion page looks at one such question: which creative occupations are exposed to generative AI.

07Method and sources

Trade data. UNCTADstat, “Creative goods: value” (US.CreativeGoodsValue), bulk download accessed 18 November 2026 (SHA-256 recorded in the repository). Exports to the world in United States dollars ($) at current prices, 2002–2024.

Groups. UN Statistics Division, Standard country or area codes (M49), for regions and for LDC, LLDC and SIDS membership (current lists). Development-status totals are UNCTADstat's own aggregates.

Coverage measure. For each current economy and year from 2002 to 2023, the year counts as missing when UNCTADstat publishes no value: the row is marked “Not publishable”, carries no value, or is absent. Rows marked “Not applicable” (for example, before an economy existed) are excluded. Former codes are joined to current ones (Sudan before 2012, Indonesia in 2002); entities that no longer exist are excluded. Nine sovereign States that appear only as trading partners, never as reporters, are counted as missing in every year: Chad, Haiti, Somalia and South Sudan (LDCs), and Equatorial Guinea, Turkmenistan, the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, the Marshall Islands and Nauru. Group differences are reported with 95% intervals from a bootstrap over economies and a one-sided permutation test. A missing value means that no figure is published. It does not mean that the economy had no creative trade, and UNCTAD's group totals include estimates for some of these economies.

HS revisions. UNCTADstat creative product groupings for HS 2002, 2007, 2012, 2017 and 2022, matched against the UNSD HS correlation tables. Each code is classed as kept, renumbered, split (1:n), merged (n:1) or complex (n:m). Complex cases where the code itself continues and only gains a partial link to a new heading are shown separately. Counts are of codes, not trade value. Sources and checksums.
